Where we come from
↑ Hover. Go on. She's been waiting.

It started the way these things do. One friend had a leak. Then a friend of that friend had a door that wouldn't shut. Then a man she had never met called her "the property lady" at a barbecue and asked her to look at his gutters. While she was eating.

Tina's idea was simple to the point of stubbornness: the place you actually live in deserves as much care as the place you post about on holiday. So she kept the good tradespeople close, kept the dodgy ones very far away, and refused to let anything half-finished out the door.

Now it's a business. Same Tina, same toolbox, but with invoices. Which, frankly, is better for everyone's friendship.

Your friends are good at everything except the bit that's actually broken.

What changed

Everyone owns a cordless drill now. Everyone has watched the video. What used to take a professional a week now takes an amateur a whole weekend, two trips to the hardware store and a quiet visit to the emergency room.

Which is why confidence stopped being the hard part. Point a man at a dripping tap and he'll give you back the average of every tap he's ever seen. Competent enough to start. Instantly flooded.

Brilliant at

Buying the property, posting the keys on Instagram, and saying "how hard can it be?"

Hopeless at

Knowing which noises a house is allowed to make, and calling someone before it gets worse.

And that last bit is where the real work lives. It's still 90 percent of the effort, and it's the part you genuinely shouldn't do yourself. Put the drill down. We've all seen your shelves.

Move your property up the levels of adulthood.

Passing on our learnings

Home ownership has levels, a bit like self-driving cars. At one end someone is watching a tutorial at 11pm with a butter knife in one hand and a torch in their mouth. At the other, the problem is fixed before you've finished panicking about it.

Most people are idling in the "call Dad" phase. Tina moves you up a level at a time, so your house stops being a hobby and goes back to being somewhere you live.

L1YouTube. You watch a tutorial. Things get worse, but now with confidence.
L3Dad. Arrives with opinions, a toolbox from 1987, and no intention of leaving before lunch.
L5Tina. One message. Fixed. You stay on the couch, where frankly you belong.

We know where the line is, and we'll happily talk you out of the feature wall that's going to embarrass you in six months.
